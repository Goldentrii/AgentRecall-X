# AgentRecall v3.4.43 Eval — Layer 3: RMR (Repeated-Mistake Reduction)

Evaluator: Sonnet L3 worker. plywood SOP `c36ba121`. Snapshot: `/tmp/ar-eval-snapshot` (read-only; real `~/.agent-recall` untouched; no MCP tools used).

This is the layer closest to AgentRecall's reason to exist: **does the memory system actually stop the agent from repeating a mistake once the rule is on file?** Graded hardest, per instruction.

---

## Pre-registered metrics (fixed before reading results)

1. Overall phantom rate = confirmed phantoms / total classified members
2. Per-class **post-encoding** recurrence (the real M4 metric — phantoms dated strictly after the class's actual re-abstraction event, re-derived independently, not the raw `phantom` flag which mixes pre-formalization and post-fix baselines)
3. Convergence evidence — which re-abstracted classes show zero repeats after their fix vs. which recurred (verify C6 independently)
4. Recurrence interval — for high-frequency classes, is the gap between same-class incidents lengthening or flat/shortening
5. Unclassified backlog risk (31 members) — sample 10, judge real-error vs noise, does it inflate or deflate true RMR

A sixth, unplanned but decisive source turned up mid-analysis: the raw `retrieved_count`/`heeded_count`/`precision` telemetry that AgentRecall's own code (`packages/core/src/storage/corrections.ts`, `check-action.ts`) writes onto every correction record. This is a second, **independent, non-curated** measurement of the same question, and it disagrees sharply with the taxonomy's story. Both are reported.

## 3. Convergence evidence — is C6 really the only 2x-recurrence case?

**No.** Verified independently from the raw correction files (not the taxonomy summary):

- **C6** — genesis 2026-05-20 → repeat 2026-07-06 (pre-fix, 47d later) → 1st re-abstraction 2026-07-16 → repeat 2026-07-30 (**14 days post-fix**, confirmed) → 2nd, deeper re-abstraction 2026-08-13. Corroborated by raw JSON: `2026-07-06-keep-it-a-patch-3-4-36.json` and `2026-07-30-a-small-version-is-enough.json` both exist, both `authoritative: true`, dates match exactly.
- **C8** — 1st re-abstraction 2026-07-14 → repeat 2026-07-21 (**7 days post-fix**, `2026-07-21-user-twice-nothing-changed-on-the-pics-a.json`, `severity: p0`, `failure_class: publish_gate`, confirmed phantom by the taxonomy itself) → 2nd, broadened re-abstraction 2026-08-13. This is the **same pattern as C6** — fix, fast repeat, second fix — but the task brief didn't name it, and the taxonomy's own history entries obscure it further: two other C8 members dated 2026-07-22 (1–8 days post-fix) were reassigned from C2 during reflect-cycle1 and had their phantom flag manually reset to `false` with the note "phantom reset on reassignment." I pulled the raw files (`2026-07-22-don-t-put-content-in-a-table...json`, `severity: p0`, `2026-07-22-part-1-is-the-distilled...json`) — both are dated strictly after C8's 07-14 rule_date and both describe the same class of failure (deliverable spec not honored) as the class they were moved into. Resetting their phantom status on reassignment is a judgment call that quietly removes 2 more post-fix data points from the non-convergence tally. Whether or not you accept that reset, C8 already has 1 confirmed post-fix repeat on its own — enough to establish the pattern independently of the disputed pair.

So: **2 of 2 classes that received a genuine second real-world test after their fix (C6, C8) recurred.** That is a 100% non-convergence rate on the only sub-sample where the claim was actually put to the test. The other 6 "clean" classes simply haven't been tested enough to say either way.

## 4. Recurrence interval — lengthening (learning) or flat?

- **C6**: genesis→repeat1 = 47 days (pre-fix). Fix (07-16) → repeat2 = **14 days**. The gap from the fix to the next incident is *shorter* than the gap that existed before any intervention — i.e. the mistake came back faster after the "cure" than it recurred spontaneously beforehand.
- **C8**: fix (07-14) → repeat = **7 days**. Same signature, even faster.
- **C1** (5 members, sorted intervals 28/17/3/43 days, median 22.5d): the last gap (43d, ending in the disputed 07-28 member) is the longest yet — mild positive signal, but rides on a member I judge misclassified.
- **C2** (8 members, sorted intervals 0/0/0/7/13/22/35, median 7d): heavily clustered same-day cross-project events early on, then 35 days of silence since the fix — improving, but small N and the clustering makes "median interval" a weak descriptor.
- **C5** (7 members, sorted intervals 0/2/3/3/3/19, median 3d): tight ~3-day clustering pre-fix, then 50 days of silence since 06-29 — the strongest genuine "lengthening" signal in the dataset.

Net read: **for the two classes where the fix was actually tested twice, the interval did not lengthen — it shortened.** That is the opposite of what "reducing repeated mistakes" should look like. The classes showing genuine lengthening (C5) never had their fix tested a second time either, so "lengthening" there is really just "silence," not a confirmed downward trend under repeated load.

## 6. Independent cross-check: raw retrieved/heeded telemetry (not curator judgment)

AgentRecall stores its own compliance signal on every correction file: `retrieved_count` ("surfaced via check/recall"), `heeded_count` ("agent's next action honored it"), `precision = heeded/retrieved`. This is defined in `packages/core/src/storage/corrections.ts` and consumed by `check-action.ts`, which documents its own noise gate: **`precision<0.3 && retrieved>=3` = "a low-signal P0 that keeps firing without being heeded."** That is the product's own words for what I'd otherwise call a phantom.

Computed across every correction file in the snapshot with `retrieved_count>0` (32 records, all projects):

- **Aggregate: 62 heeded / 491 retrieved = 12.6% heed rate.**
- **19 of 32 retrieved corrections (59%) cross the product's own noise/non-compliance threshold** (precision<0.3, retrieved≥3) — including the highest-traffic, most general CLAUDE.md-level rules: "don't map to human memory" (64 retrieved / 10 heeded, 15.6%), "use Chinese when asked" (55/2, 3.6%), "never dispatch sub-agents on Fable" (49/4, 8.2%), "decision menus" (21/5, 23.8%), "never reveal margin/cost-basis" (18/5, 27.8%), "don't use personal Supabase for product data" (18/5, 27.8%).
- The handful of corrections with precision near 1.0 all have `retrieved_count=1` — i.e. they've only been tested once; the pattern of decay appears specifically on the rules that get retrieved often, which is exactly the population that matters for RMR (broad, recurring-context rules), not a random subset.

This is a materially worse picture than the curated taxonomy's ~50% phantom rate, and it isn't subject to the same "no test opportunity" excuse — every one of these 32 records was, by definition, retrieved as relevant at least once. The gap between this number (12.6% heeded) and the taxonomy's implied "6/8 classes look clean" story is itself a finding: the `/arreflect` self-assessment process appears to run somewhat optimistic relative to the product's own raw usage logs.

Caveat: low `heeded_count` conflates two possible causes — (a) the agent saw the rule and ignored it (true RMR failure) vs (b) the retrieval engine over-fired and surfaced a rule that wasn't actually applicable that turn (retrieval-precision noise, not behavioral failure). I can't fully separate these without deeper session-level replay, which is out of scope here. But the code's own comment ("keeps firing without being heeded") frames this metric as a compliance signal, not a retrieval-quality signal, and the concentration of low precision on exactly the broad, general-purpose rules (rather than narrow, rarely-applicable ones) is consistent with (a), not (b).

## M4 Verdict

**Partial, leaning toward the product's core claim NOT holding up under its own dogfood data.**

- The manually-curated taxonomy, read charitably, shows 6 of 8 tested classes with zero recorded post-fix repeats — but for 4 of those 6 the sample size is 2–3 members and the "clean" record is really "hasn't been tested again yet," not "learned."
- The 2 classes that *did* get tested a second time (C6, C8) **both recurred**, both within 7–14 days of their fix, and both required a second, deeper rewrite on the same day (2026-08-13) — a 100% failure rate on the only real second-attempt sample.
- The recurrence interval for those two classes **shortened**, not lengthened, after intervention — the opposite of a learning signal.
- The one class with a genuinely clean, well-tested convergence (C3) is enforced by a **hard PreToolUse hook**, not memory recall — i.e. the cleanest win in the dataset is evidence for code-level guardrails, not for the memory mechanism per se.
- The unclassified backlog hides an estimated 18-19 additional real error-class corrections that the taxonomy has never counted, at least one of which (decision-menus) is independently confirmed by raw telemetry to be a live, frequently-surfaced, mostly-ignored rule (23.8% heed rate over 21 retrievals).
- The product's own retrieved/heeded telemetry — an independent, non-curated signal — shows a **12.6% aggregate heed rate**, and by the product's own internal noise/non-compliance gate, **59% of all actively-retrieved corrections "keep firing without being heeded."**

Put plainly: when a rule is written down once and never tested again, AgentRecall looks fine. When a rule is actually exercised repeatedly (version bumps, UI specs, decision-menu formatting, "don't map to human memory," language preference), the hit rate for actually preventing the repeat is low — roughly 1 in 8 by the raw telemetry, and 0-for-2 by the taxonomy's own hardest test. This is the opposite of what "measurably reducing repeat mistakes" should show. The mechanism (capture, storage, retrieval, injection) is solid — that's a different eval layer's job — but **the closed loop that's supposed to convert a stored correction into changed behavior is not demonstrated to work reliably in this codebase's own data.**

## Proposed grade: **PARTIAL / WEAK-PASS at best — do not certify RMR as proven**

Rationale: I am not grading down to an outright FAIL because (a) the mechanism clearly does *something* — several classes show long silences after fixes, and the one hook-enforced class is genuinely clean; (b) the sample sizes for most classes are too small (2-3 members) to declare confident failure either; (c) the owner-driven reflect cycles are actively re-abstracting and tightening rules in response to real recurrence, which is the intended feedback loop working as designed, just not yet converging. But I am not grading this a PASS, because the two highest-traffic, most-tested classes both failed their first fix, the raw independent telemetry shows an 87% non-heed rate in aggregate, and the curation process itself shows signs of softening the count (the C8 "phantom reset on reassignment" judgment call). If forced onto a FATAL/DEGRADED/CLEAN-style scale: **DEGRADED** — the product does something, but the core "stops repeat mistakes" claim is not supported by its own data at the fidelity this eval demands.
